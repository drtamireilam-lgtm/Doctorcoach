# DoctorCoach — Marketing & Product Growth Roadmap

This document converts current product ideas into an execution roadmap. It is intentionally focused on a simple promise: **injury → assessment → rehabilitation → coach-built training → optional nutrition → measurable progress, all in one place.**

## 1. Marketing content system

### A. Patient-journey videos
Create short vertical videos around one clear problem at a time: shoulder, knee, lower back, elbow, hip, etc.

Recommended sequence:
1. Pain interrupts training.
2. Athlete opens DoctorCoach.
3. Athlete books a medical assessment.
4. Dr. Tamir Eilam assesses the athlete.
5. A rehabilitation direction is set.
6. Nadav Ron builds/adapts the training plan.
7. Optional nutrition support from Lior Zelikson.
8. App scroll shows the actual flow.
9. Progress improves over time.
10. CTA: book / start through DoctorCoach.

### B. “What happens after an injury?” shorts
15–25 second videos. Hook with one real training problem and show that the answer is not simply “ignore it” or “stop all training”. Emphasize guided assessment and adaptation rather than diagnosis by the app.

### C. Team-expertise series
Four recurring formats:
- “What the doctor looks for” — Dr. Tamir Eilam.
- “What the coach changes” — Nadav Ron, Mr. Israel 2024 and certified fitness coach.
- “What the dietitian adds” — Lior Zelikson, clinical dietitian.
- “Three professionals, one shared plan” — team video.

### D. Real app screen recordings
Use actual screen recording rather than concept renders whenever possible. Suggested path:
Dashboard → pain map → medical review → rehabilitation → training → RPE/RIR → progress → team.

### E. Data-based success stories
Use consented, non-identifying outcomes such as pain trend, ROM, working load, e1RM, adherence, readiness or return-to-training milestones. Avoid guaranteed recovery claims.

## 2. Product UX direction

### A. Dashboard should answer one question
**“What do I need to do today?”**

The home screen should prioritize:
- Today’s workout or rehabilitation task.
- Current pain/safety status.
- Team task or update.
- Upcoming appointment.
- Progress shortcut.

Avoid presenting every module as an equal first-level choice.

### B. Next Best Action
Future rule engine should surface one clear next action based on known state. Examples:
- Red flag → medical review.
- New program version → review coach update.
- Missed weight entry → log body weight.
- Elevated pain → complete medical/rehab check-in before training.

This is navigation guidance, not automated diagnosis.

### C. Show ownership everywhere
Every clinical/training/nutrition plan or update should visibly show who created or last modified it.

Suggested labels:
- Medical assessment — Dr. Tamir Eilam.
- Training plan — Nadav Ron.
- Nutrition plan — Lior Zelikson.

This reinforces that DoctorCoach coordinates real professionals rather than generating anonymous app output.

### D. Injury journey / milestones
Create a clear longitudinal view such as:
- Week 1 — pain 7/10.
- Week 3 — improved ROM.
- Week 5 — returned to selected lifts.
- Week 8 — return-to-training milestone.

Use real recorded data where available.

### E. Coach change summaries
When a coach updates a program, show a concise difference view:
- Exercise changed.
- Load/reps changed.
- Range of motion restriction changed.
- Coach note added.

The trainee should never have to guess what changed.

### F. Exercise-specific coach notes
Allow instructions such as:
- “Stop at this ROM.”
- “Use this grip.”
- “Keep pain below the agreed threshold.”
- “Send a video of set 2.”

### G. Video review with timestamped comments
Future version: coach can attach comments to exact moments in a set video, e.g. 00:07 or 00:11. This is higher-value than a generic review note.

### H. Nutrition should stay intentionally simple
Do not turn DoctorCoach into a full calorie-tracking ecosystem. Prioritize:
- Assessment.
- Plan.
- Training/rest-day guidance.
- Daily adherence.
- Body weight.
- Weekly check-in.
- Dietitian review.

## 3. Features to avoid or delay

Until the core flow is proven, deprioritize:
- Public social feed.
- Complex gamification.
- Large badge systems.
- AI injury diagnosis.
- Injury-prediction promises.
- Huge questionnaire libraries without validated/licensed use.
- Thousands of exercises before core exercise coverage is complete.
- Excessive athlete self-configuration in coached mode.

The coached experience should feel like: **“my team has already decided what I need to do next.”**

## 4. Service packaging ideas

Potential simple product structure:

### Medical
Medical assessment and direction.

### DoctorCoach Coaching
Medical direction + rehabilitation/training plan + monitoring.

### Complete
Medical + training/rehabilitation + nutrition.

### Self-use
Separate lower-cost subscription for users who explicitly want self-programming. Keep it visually and commercially distinct from professional coaching so it does not weaken the value of guided care.

## 5. Immediate execution priorities

1. Replace the old module-heavy home experience with a “Today / next action” dashboard.
2. Show professional ownership throughout the product.
3. Build the injury journey / milestone timeline.
4. Add coach-update summaries and per-exercise notes.
5. Improve progress storytelling so it can support both user motivation and consented marketing case studies.
6. Create real app-screen-recording marketing assets once the main flows are stable.

## 6. Marketing language guardrails

Prefer:
- assessment
- guided rehabilitation
- coach-built training
- clinical nutrition support
- progress tracking
- coordinated professional team

Avoid unsupported claims such as:
- “AI diagnosis”
- “prevents injuries”
- “guaranteed recovery”
- “pain-free in X days”

DoctorCoach should be marketed as a coordinated professional pathway, not as an automated diagnostic tool.
